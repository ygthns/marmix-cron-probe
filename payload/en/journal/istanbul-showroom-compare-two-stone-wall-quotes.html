After an Istanbul showroom visit, how do you compare two stone wall quotations?

Compare Anora travertine and Sesmari limestone on one elevation. Separate visible area, purchased pieces, finished edges, samples and delivery before deciding.

MARMIX JOURNAL2026-11-234 min read

Why two square-metre rates can describe different orders

You may leave an Istanbul natural stone showroom with two favourite patterns: a repeating travertine panel and a larger limestone motif. Giving both suppliers the same wall area does not settle how many pieces you need. A narrow band above a doorway might require a full panel of one pattern, while an offcut from another row might complete the other. Draw Marmix Anora and Sesmari Limra against the same elevation. Keep one column for what the visitor will see and another for the pieces the buyer must order.

Record what would actually change the material decision

Place the samples side by side in lighting close to the final interior. Keep the viewing distance, floor colour and adjacent furniture the same. Readability of the motif, natural tone and how the surface feels to the touch are separate judgements. A preference for one strongly veined sample does not establish that every panel will have equally dense veining. Stone Federation Great Britain discusses reviewing a range of samples when selecting natural stone. The Istanbul showroom visit guide covers measurements and photographs to bring.

Put scope differences into words before comparing totals

Suppose one quotation includes factory-finished exposed edges, while the other supplies full panels for site cutting. In the first case edge work may sit within the panel rate. In the second, the installer may need to price cutting, handling and time on site. Ask both suppliers for the number of full pieces, quantity of special finishes, treatment of the sample charge when ordering and the exact delivery point. An empty entry is an open question for the supplier. Leaving it visible makes the financial comparison more honest than treating it as a zero.

Tie the final choice to a place on the drawing

Pattern continuity might matter most at the entrance, while efficient use of material may matter more behind fixed furniture. Mark those decisions on the elevation and keep it with the dated quotation and sample photographs. The Işık Maden and Marmix production record gives the manufacturing background for the brand. Confirm which operations can be carried out for the wall in question and how they affect its price. Sending the same drawing with the bespoke CNC panel sample and quotation brief also makes changes in quantity and cost easier to trace on the next revision.

Calculate the face area, then draw the cuts

Consider a wall 3.60 metres wide and 2.70 metres high. Its gross area is 9.72 square metres. Subtract a doorway measuring 0.90 by 2.10 metres and the flat face to be clad is 7.83 square metres. Multiplying that number by a square-metre rate gives only a first estimate. Do the panels return into the opening? Should the motif be centred over the lintel? Can an offcut be reused at the opposite end? Mark the answers on the drawing for each pattern. The full-piece quantities for the two patterns may legitimately differ even though the visible area is identical.

Take the open decisions home from the showroom

Use one line on a decision sheet for your preferred stone and another for the alternative still in contention. Record the sample reference, edge treatment, delivery scope and quotation validity on each line. Leave the last column for what could change the decision. A pattern that looks too prominent in the actual room could change the material choice. A delivery price that stops at the building entrance could change the handling budget. Recording those separately allows the designer and buyer to resolve the right question. When the decision is made, send the supplier a marked copy of the same sheet rather than starting a new email trail from memory.

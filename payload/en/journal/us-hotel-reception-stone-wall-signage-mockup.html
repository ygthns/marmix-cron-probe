How to review a carved marble backdrop for a US hotel reception

Test the hotel name, carved marble and lighting together. A three-view mock-up helps US project teams review Marmix Torsion before ordering the reception wall.

MARMIX JOURNAL2026-11-234 min read

Start at the entrance, where guests first read the sign

The marble behind a boutique hotel reception desk and the hotel lettering often come from different suppliers. Until they meet, a shadow from the stone relief can fall straight through a thin letter. For a US architect sourcing CNC-carved stone panels from Türkiye, a room elevation and a stone name leave that question open. Mark the entrance, the queue and the front of the desk on the plan. From each position, establish where a stroke of the lettering crosses a panel joint. This is a useful decision to make while the wall still exists on paper.

Review a few full-size letters with the stone

Directional relief on a panel such as Torsion Green or Torsion Grey catches light differently along either edge. Put two or three full-size letters with a representative finish in front of the physical sample on a temporary display. A word that reads clearly from the queue can dissolve into the pattern when viewed from the doorway. Photograph each view under daylight and evening lighting with consistent camera settings. Note the distance and light direction. Stone Federation Great Britain describes why one indicative sample may not show the range of the full order. See the comparison of grey and green Torsion marble for the material choices.

Give the fabricator and sign installer the same revision

Look for fixings that coincide with a joint, a shallow part of the relief or an access panel. The responsible design team should establish the load-bearing support and fixing detail. What the stone fabricator needs is an approved drawing that clearly identifies any holes or cut-outs within its scope. If site dimensions arrive in inches and shop drawings use millimetres, identify the governing unit and draw from one reference point. A small rounding error repeated across several letters can visibly shift the sign away from the desk centreline.

Ask for separate costs and an explicit delivery point

Request separate allowances for the panels, bespoke machining, approval sample, packing and delivery to the quoted destination. Identify who owns the final site survey, lettering and lighting. The Işık Maden and Marmix production record provides context for the Marmix production process. If the project requires US stock, local installation or a particular arrival date, have each service confirmed for the actual quantity and address. The guide to sourcing stone panels for US projects explains what to send with the enquiry.

Record why the mock-up was accepted

Use separate lines for the guest at the front desk and the visitor standing in the doorway. At each position, note lettering legibility, the joint nearest the sign and the shadows under evening lighting. A comment such as “the first two letters merge with the relief shadow from the entrance” tells the designer what to change. If moving the light solves the issue, the team can limit the next review to the changed component. Date the accepted elevation and decide who will retain the physical reference. A photograph alone may be difficult to interpret after the original lighting arrangement has been dismantled.

Decide what governs if the site survey changes

Suppose the completed desk centreline falls slightly away from its design position. Shifting the panels, joints and letters together may not produce the intended result. Should the hotel name align with the desk, or with the two openings framing the wall? Have the architect mark the governing line and the sign fabricator update its fixing template. Confirm both drawing revisions in the same approval before releasing the stone. This small coordination step helps avoid the stone workshop cutting to an old sign while the sign maker works to a newer wall.

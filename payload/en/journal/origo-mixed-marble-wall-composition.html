How to combine different marbles on one wall with Origo

Compare Leylak Vişne, Verde Guatemala and grey marble in Origo. Choose a dominant stone, map the motifs and review samples for a bespoke natural stone wall.

MARMIX JOURNAL2026-11-232 min read

Choose a dominant stone and accent colours

When combining different natural stones and motifs on a single wall, decide which colour should dominate from the main viewing point. Marmix’s Origo collection features Leylak Vişne, Verde Guatemala and grey marble, with 10 motif options. The circular forms frame the veining in each stone. For a quieter composition, use grey as a recurring colour and group the green or burgundy accents at selected points. See Torsion Green for a comparison with geometric relief in a single colour.

Set out the sequence on a wall elevation

Start with Origo’s 2 × 14 × 28 cm panel size and mark the clear wall dimensions, doorways, sockets, furniture lines and main viewing direction on the elevation. Test the recurring motif in the most visible part of the wall first. At the edges, mark where cuts would interrupt a circle. When a reference photograph is the starting point, our guide to turning a reference photo into a bespoke stone wall explains what to capture in the drawing.

Lay the stones side by side and compare them in daylight

One piece of each colour can miss variation in tone and veining. For a broad wall, lay several pieces out in the proposed order. Photograph them in light similar to the room and from the intended viewing distance. Pay particular attention to how the grey changes the transition between the two accent colours. Stone Federation Great Britain distinguishes an indicative sample from a set of samples that shows the natural range of colour and texture. Our natural stone wall sample guide covers what else to look for when reviewing the pieces in person.

Share the drawing when enquiring about bespoke CNC panels

If you are sourcing bespoke natural stone wall panels for an architectural project, send Marmix the wall elevation, your preferred Origo motifs and the stones you want to emphasise. Where the motif sequence or dimensions change, identify the pieces to be cut on the drawing. The standard collection and project-specific CNC machining options can then be discussed separately. Our guide to CNC stone panel samples and quotations sets out the details to include with your enquiry.

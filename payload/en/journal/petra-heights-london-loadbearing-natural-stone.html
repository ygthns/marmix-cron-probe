The London apartments held up by natural stone

At Petra Heights in north-west London, loadbearing lava stone has put natural stone’s role in multi-storey buildings back in the spotlight.

MARMIX JOURNAL2026-11-241 min read

Why is Petra Heights in the news?

Petra Heights on Finchley Road in north-west London was covered this month by Smithsonian Magazine and The Architect’s Newspaper. Structural engineer Webb Yates describes the roughly 3,000-square-metre project, designed with Groupwork, as 22 homes above workspace on the lower levels. Lava stone forms its loadbearing exoskeleton. Webb Yates Engineers · Petra Heights Smithsonian Magazine

How does loadbearing stone differ from cladding?

Cladding passes the forces acting on it, such as wind, through its fixings to the structure behind it. A loadbearing exoskeleton participates directly in transferring the building’s loads. At Petra Heights, that role belongs to the stone elements visible outside. Stone selection and connection design are therefore considered alongside the appearance of the façade.

What belongs in the carbon comparison?

Assessing a loadbearing stone system means looking at the whole chain from quarry block to installation. Quarry and fabrication yields, transport distance, connection hardware and the building’s other structural components all affect the comparison. Material systems should be compared for the same function and over the same life-cycle boundaries. For cladding drawings and samples, the Journal’s stone cladding specification guide sets out a different set of questions.
